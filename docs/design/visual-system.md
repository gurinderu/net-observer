## 1. What is wrong today, stated as failures

The owner's observations on live data, restated as design failures so each has a
rule to answer it:

| Observation | Failure | Answered by |
|---|---|---|
| The windows look like the work of different people | No shared scale — 62 bare `text_size` literals across **seven** sizes (9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15) and only two named constants | §3, §5, §14 |
| Almost everything is boxed | See §1.1 — the diagnosis is *not* borders | §7 |
| Numbers shake on the 3-second refresh | Proportional figures; digit widths differ | §4 |
| Hypothesis warnings are paragraphs of orange | Epistemics carried by prose and alarm colour | §11 |
| Role glyphs are at mismatched sizes | Glyphs sized ad hoc, not on the scale | §3.4 |
| Empty states look like broken layout | No designed empty form | §12 |

### 1.1 Correcting the "everything is boxed" diagnosis

An inventory of the current code found **four** `border_1()` call sites in the
whole app: the panel rim (`ui.rs:620`), the flyout menu rim (`menu.rs:313`), the
non-gateway neighbour chip (`map.rs:384`), and the uplink card (`map.rs:735`).
So the *boxed* feeling is not literally borders. It comes from three other
sources, and the rules below target those instead:

1. **Chips.** `rounded_md` filled-and-outlined chips are the default way of
   presenting a neighbour, a filter, a tab. A grid of chips reads as a grid of
   boxes even when only one of them has a stroke.
2. **Hairline separators.** `separator()` is copy-pasted four times
   (`ui.rs:1305`, `map.rs:1120`, `air.rs:1470`, `events.rs:467`) and every
   section is delimited by one, full-bleed. Full-bleed rules between every block
   compartmentalise a window as effectively as borders do.
3. **Three unrelated ways to draw a 1px line** — an `h(px(1.0))` div, an
   `h(px(AXIS_RULE_H))` div, and `PathBuilder::stroke(px(1.0))` — which do not
   land on the same pixel or the same colour.

§7 therefore attacks separators and chips, not just `border_1`.

### 1.2 What does not exist yet

Two things the brief assumed are not in the code, and the spec has to say so
rather than pretend to reform them:

- **There are no signal rings.** RSSI is encoded *only* as bar opacity
  (`weight()`, `air.rs:615`, mapping −90…−35 dBm onto α 0.25…0.85, and α 0.20
  for "not reported"). §10.6 argues this is a defect, not a style, and proposes
  the `Arc` mark as its replacement.
- **There is no animation of any kind** — no `with_animation`, no easing,
  no interpolated state; hover is instant. §13 is therefore mostly a rule for
  keeping it that way, plus three additions.

## 2. The instrument thesis

This is a forensic instrument, not a dashboard. Three consequences drive every
number below.

**A. The reading is the subject; the chrome is not.** Ink goes to measurements.
Containers, separators, labels and frames get the smallest weight that still
does the job. Anything that draws the eye and is not a measurement is a bug.

**B. Colour is a claim, not decoration.** If something is red, the daemon is
asserting a failure it measured. A red thing that is merely *suspected* is a
lie of exactly the kind this project exists to prevent. Hence §11: uncertainty
is carried by **form**, not by hue, and never by an orange paragraph.

**C. Silence must be visibly bracketed.** `SKIP`, quiet mode, operator pause and
"this daemon cannot do that" are four different absences. The eye must be able
to tell them apart at a glance, in a 320px column, without reading. §11.4.

## 3. Type scale

### 3.1 The scale

Five sizes. Nothing else may appear anywhere in the codebase.

| Token | px | Weight | Tracking | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `type.micro` | 11 | 510 (medium) | +0.2 | Axis tick labels, unit suffixes, chip text, timestamps in dense rows |
| `type.body` | 13 | 400 (regular) | 0 | Default. Every list row, every label, every sentence |
| `type.strong` | 15 | 590 (semibold) | −0.1 | Section headers, the single answer of a card |
| `type.figure` | 20 | 500 (medium) | −0.3 | The primary reading of a card (latency, loss, channel) |
| `type.hero` | 28 | 600 (semibold) | −0.5 | Exactly one per surface: the panel's verdict line. Nowhere else |

Line height: **`ceil(size × 1.35)` rounded to the 2px grid** → 15 / 18 / 21 / 27 / 38.
Hero and figure lines are set to `1.15` instead (32 / 23) because a large number
sitting in a 1.35 box floats and reads as misaligned against its label.

### 3.2 Why 13 and not 12

The panel hangs off the macOS menu bar and sits three pixels from native menus.
`NSFont.systemFontSize` is **13pt**, and AppKit menu items render at 13–14pt.
A 12px body would be *systematically one step smaller* than every native
control the user sees in the same glance, which reads not as "compact" but as
"caption" — the whole app would look like a footnote to the system. 13 makes the
panel a peer of the menu bar. It is also the size at which SF's digit forms hold
their stroke at 1× on a non-retina external display, which this machine has.

12 is additionally rejected because it forces `type.micro` to 10 to keep a
perceptible step, and 10px SF loses the crossbar distinction between `6`/`8` in
tabular figures at 1× — fatal for a number the user is going to quote in an
argument with the network's operator.

### 3.3 Why five sizes and these ratios

Steps are 11 → 13 (1.18), 13 → 15 (1.15), 15 → 20 (1.33), 20 → 28 (1.40).
The two small steps are deliberately *sub-perceptual as sizes* — 11 and 13 are
told apart by weight and colour, not by size, so that a dense row does not
visibly stripe. The two large steps are supra-perceptual: a figure must never be
mistakable for a header. A geometric scale (e.g. 1.25 throughout) would put a
size at 16.25 between header and figure, and every implementer would round it
differently. Integers only, no derived sizes, no `size * 0.9`.

### 3.4 Glyphs

Role glyphs (the emoji/symbol marks on map nodes and menu rows) are **not** text
and must not inherit text size. Rule:

- A glyph is drawn in a **square box** whose side is a scale value: 16, 20 or 28.
- The glyph's own point size is **box × 0.72**, rounded down: 11 / 14 / 20.
- Box side is chosen by the row it lives in, never by the glyph: dense list row →
  16; map node → 20; card header → 28.
- The box is centred on the row's optical centre line, not on its text baseline.

That is the whole fix for "glyphs of different sizes": the glyph never chooses
its own size, the container does.

Measured today, the same four role glyphs (`⇅` gateway, `▣` infra, `●` host,
`?` unknown, `role_glyph` at `map.rs:293`) are drawn at **10px** in the node chip
(`map.rs:399`), at an **inherited 13px** in the neighbour-list role column
(`map.rs:1044` — no `text_size` call at all), and at **10px** in the legend
(`map.rs:900`). Meanwhile the uplink markers `●`/`■` are at **9px**
(`map.rs:641`, `map.rs:740`), the panel health dot `●` at **10px**
(`ui.rs:693`), and the offline `⚠` at **13px** (`ui.rs:751`). Six sizes for what
is visually one family of marks. Under the rule above: list row → 16 box / 11pt;
map node → 20 box / 14pt; panel header dot → 20 box / 14pt; legend → 16 box.

Note also that `⇅ ▣ ● ?` are four glyphs from four different Unicode blocks with
four different optical weights and, at 10px, four different apparent sizes even
when nominally equal. The 0.72 rule fixes the *box*; making them look equal
additionally requires a per-glyph optical scale factor, which an implementer must
tune by eye against a rendered screenshot — one of the things this agent cannot
observe (see §16).

## 4. Numerals: the jitter fix

The shaking is not a refresh problem. `48 ms` and `113 ms` in proportional SF
have different advance widths per digit, so every 3-second tick reflows the row.

**Rule N1 — every digit that can change is set in tabular figures.**
In gpui: the numeric font feature `tnum` on the SF face, or `.font_family("SF Mono")`
for the figure sizes. Applies to: latencies, loss %, RSSI, channel numbers,
counts, byte sizes, timestamps, MAC/IP octets.

**Rule N2 — reserve the maximum width, not the current width.**
A field renders inside a fixed-width slot sized for its worst case
(`ch × max_digits`), right-aligned, so a 3-digit value replacing a 2-digit value
does not move its neighbours. Slot widths in `ch` units of the tabular face:

| Quantity | Format | Slot |
|---|---|---|
| Latency | `%.0f` ms, 0–9999 | 4ch + unit |
| Loss | `%.0f` %, 0–100 | 3ch + unit |
| RSSI | `−%d` dBm, 0–120 | 4ch + unit |
| Channel | `%d`, 1–196 | 3ch |
| Count | `%d`, 0–99999 | 5ch |
| Age | `%ds` / `%dm` / `%dh` | 3ch |

**Rule N3 — the unit is not part of the number.** Unit suffix is `type.micro`,
`ink.tertiary`, in its own slot, left-aligned after the numeral slot. It never
changes, so it never moves. This also means the eye locks onto a fixed column of
unit text and reads the varying digits against it.

**Rule N4 — no more precision than the measurement has.** Latency to the
millisecond, never a decimal. Loss to the percent. A rendered decimal that the
collector does not actually resolve is a quiet lie.

## 5. Spacing grid

Base unit **4px**. Allowed values, and only these:

| Token | px | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `space.hair` | 2 | Between a numeral and its unit; inside a chip |
| `space.tight` | 4 | Between a label and its value in the same row |
| `space.snug` | 8 | Between rows of a list; icon-to-text |
| `space.base` | 12 | Between related blocks inside a card |
| `space.gutter` | 16 | Window edge inset; between cards |
| `space.section` | 24 | Between top-level sections; above a section header |

Why 4 and not 8: the panel is 320px wide, and 8px granularity forces either
16px gutters with a 288 content column (workable) *or* every internal gap to be
8 or 16 (too coarse — the gap between a label and its value must be smaller than
the gap between two rows, or a two-column list reads as one column). 4 gives the
needed intermediate steps while still landing every edge on an even pixel.

**Panel geometry, fixed:**

```
320 total
 16 gutter │        288 content        │ 16 gutter
```

Card internal padding is `space.base` (12) top/bottom, `space.base` (12)
left/right. A card thus has a 264px inner column. Any plot inside a card is
264 wide — that number is a constant, not a measurement, so sparklines across
different cards align pixel-for-pixel down the panel.

**Vertical rhythm.** Every row's height is a multiple of 4 and is one of:
24 (dense), 32 (standard), 44 (touch/primary action). No free-height rows.

## 6. Radii

| Token | px | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `radius.none` | 0 | Plot areas, separators, table cells |
| `radius.chip` | 5 | Chips, badges, the epistemic markers of §11 |
| `radius.card` | 9 | Cards inside a window |
| `radius.window` | 12 | Panel and window shells |

Nesting rule: an inner radius equals `outer − padding`, clamped to the table.
Card (9) inside a window (12) with 12 padding would compute to 0; we clamp to 9
because a square card inside a rounded window reads as a rendering error. Chips
inside a card at 12 padding compute to −3 → clamp to 5.

macOS draws continuous ("squircle") corners; gpui's `rounded_*` is circular. At
these radii the difference is under one pixel, so circular is accepted. At 12 it
is visible on a large fill — hence the window shell should use the platform
window rounding rather than drawing its own.

## 7. Line weights, and the abolition of the border

| Token | px | Colour token | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stroke.hair` | 1 | `line.hair` | Separators inside a list |
| `stroke.edge` | 1 | `line.edge` | The three sanctioned borders below |
| `stroke.data` | 1.5 | data colour | Sparkline, ring, axis, star edge |
| `stroke.emphasis` | 2 | data colour | The selected/hovered data series only |
| `stroke.focus` | 2 | `accent` | Keyboard focus ring, offset 2px outside |

### The border rule

**A border may be drawn in exactly three situations. Everywhere else, grouping
is expressed by spacing and by a background fill.**

1. **The window shell.** One `line.edge` hairline around the panel, because it
   floats over arbitrary desktop content and needs a terminating edge.
2. **A container that can be focused or acted on** (a button, a text field, a
   selectable map node) — and only in its focused/hovered state.
3. **A plot's baseline axis**, which is data, not chrome.

Everything currently boxed becomes:

| Was | Becomes |
|---|---|
| Bordered card | Fill `surface.raised` (§8), `radius.card`, no border |
| Bordered section | `space.section` above + a `type.strong` header, no border, no rule line |
| Bordered list | Rows separated by `line.hair` **inset to the text column**, not full-bleed, and only between rows — no line above the first or below the last |
| Bordered stat tile | Nothing. Grid gap `space.base` is the separation |
| Bordered warning block | See §11 — it stops being a block |

### The separator rule

Equally important, given §1.1. **One `separator()`**, in one place, with one
appearance — the four copies collapse to a single shared helper.

- A separator is **inset to the text column**, never full-bleed: it starts at the
  content's leading edge and ends at its trailing edge.
- It appears **between** sibling rows only. Never above the first row, never
  below the last, never between a header and its content (that gap is
  `space.base`, not a line).
- **A separator may not be used to delimit sections.** Sections are separated by
  `space.section` (24) and named by a `type.strong` header. This deletes most of
  the current rules and is the second-largest contributor to the four windows
  looking like one instrument.

### The chip rule

A chip is a *value*, not a container. Therefore:

- A chip has a fill (`surface.raised`, or `accent` when selected) and
  `radius.chip`, and **no border** — replacing `map.rs:384` and the tab/filter
  chips in `map.rs:1224`, `air.rs:1123`, `events.rs:391`.
- The one exception is the gateway/uplink emphasis, which currently uses a
  `track_on`-coloured border (`map.rs:736`). That becomes a **2px leading edge
  bar** inside the chip rather than a full outline: same emphasis, one quarter
  of the ink, and it does not read as a box.

Rationale: a border costs the same visual weight as a data stroke
(`stroke.data` is 1.5 vs a 1px border) — so a screen full of borders is a screen
where the chrome outweighs the measurements, which violates §2.A directly. This
is the single change that will do most to make the four windows look like one
instrument.

## 8. Colour semantics

### 8.1 The vitrail problem

The panel sits on `NSVisualEffectView` blur. An opaque background kills it; a
too-transparent one makes text contrast depend on the user's wallpaper, which is
uncontrolled and therefore unverifiable. The resolution is a **scrim**: a
near-opaque tinted layer that clamps the backdrop's luminance into a narrow
band, leaving just enough translucency for the material to read as glass.

- Dark: scrim `#1C1C1E` at **α 0.94** over the blur.
- Light: scrim `#FFFFFF` at **α 0.92** over the blur.

Composited against the three worst backdrops (black desktop, mid grey, white
window), the resulting base ranges are:

| Theme | over black | over mid grey | over white |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dark | `#1A1A1C` | `#222224` | `#2A2A2C` |
| Light | `#EBEBEB` | `#F4F4F4` | `#FFFFFF` |

That is a luminance band narrow enough that every foreground below clears
4.5:1 against *all three* — see §9. The translucency is real (6–8% of the
backdrop shows through, and the blur's own colour shift shows through fully),
so the material still reads as glass while contrast is guaranteed.

**Layer tokens** are defined as alpha over the composited base, not as opaque
colours, so they inherit the vitrail:

| Token | Dark | Light | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `surface.scrim` | `#1C1C1E` α.94 | `#FFFFFF` α.92 | The window base, over blur |
| `surface.raised` | `#FFFFFF` α.06 | `#000000` α.04 | Cards. Replaces every card border |
| `surface.sunken` | `#000000` α.20 | `#000000` α.06 | Plot wells, code/SQL fields |
| `surface.hover` | `#FFFFFF` α.09 | `#000000` α.06 | Row hover |
| `surface.active` | `#FFFFFF` α.14 | `#000000` α.10 | Row pressed/selected |
| `line.hair` | `#FFFFFF` α.10 | `#000000` α.10 | Inset row separators |
| `line.edge` | `#FFFFFF` α.16 | `#000000` α.16 | The three sanctioned borders |

Alpha layers, not opaque greys, because an opaque `#2A2A2C` card would punch a
matte hole in the glass and is the thing that "kills the blur".

### 8.2 Foreground semantics

Five roles, and every one of them is a *claim about the measurement*, not a mood.

| Token | Means | Dark | Light |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ink.primary` | The reading itself | `#F2F2F7` | `#1C1C1E` |
| `ink.secondary` | Labels, units the reading needs | `#AEAEB6` | `#55555C` |
| `ink.tertiary` | Chrome, axis ticks, "as of" | `#9A9AA4` | `#5F5F68` |
| `state.ok` | Measured, and within the expected band | `#32D74B` | `#12662F` |
| `state.warn` | Measured, and outside the band, not yet failing | `#FFD60A` | `#7A4A00` |
| `state.fail` | Measured a failure | `#FF7B72` | `#A81E14` |
| `state.skip` | Not measured — the probe could not run | `#9A9AA4` | `#5F5F68` |
| `accent` | Interactive affordance, focus, selection | `#5AC8FA` | `#0A4FA0` |

Three rules that make this semantic rather than decorative:

- **`state.skip` is deliberately identical to `ink.tertiary`.** A skip is not a
  colour of its own, because it is not a state of the network — it is a state of
  our knowledge. Its distinguishing mark is **form** (§11.4), not hue.
- **A state colour may only be applied to a value the daemon actually measured
  in the current sample.** A derived, inferred or hypothesised value never gets
  `state.*`. That is the enforcement point for §2.B.
- **`state.warn` in light theme is a dark amber-brown, not orange.** Saturated
  orange at 4.5:1 on a light background does not exist; every light-theme
  "orange warning" in a UI is either illegible or is quietly failing contrast.
  `#7A4A00` reads as amber in context (it sits beside green and red) and clears
  contrast honestly.
- **Never encode a state by hue alone.** Every state carries a shape token from
  §11.3 as well, for the ~8% of male users with a red/green deficiency and for
  the greyscale screenshot that ends up in the argument with the ISP.

## 9. Contrast, computed

WCAG 2.1 relative luminance, computed (not eyeballed) with the script in
`scratchpad/contrast.py`; reproduced here so an implementer can re-derive it.
Each cell is the ratio of the token against the *composited* base for that
backdrop, i.e. the worst case the vitrail can produce.

**Dark theme** (bases `#1A1A1C` / `#222224` / `#2A2A2C`):

| Token | hex | vs black bg | vs mid bg | vs white bg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ink.primary` | `#F2F2F7` | 15.57 | 14.23 | **12.84** |
| `ink.secondary` | `#AEAEB6` | 7.89 | 7.21 | **6.50** |
| `ink.tertiary` | `#9A9AA4` | 6.23 | 5.70 | **5.14** |
| `state.ok` | `#32D74B` | 9.07 | 8.29 | **7.47** |
| `state.warn` | `#FFD60A` | 12.31 | 11.25 | **10.15** |
| `state.fail` | `#FF7B72` | 6.89 | 6.30 | **5.68** |
| `accent` | `#5AC8FA` | 9.17 | 8.38 | **7.56** |

**Light theme** (bases `#EBEBEB` / `#F4F4F4` / `#FFFFFF`):

| Token | hex | vs black bg | vs mid bg | vs white bg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ink.primary` | `#1C1C1E` | **14.27** | 15.47 | 17.01 |
| `ink.secondary` | `#55555C` | **6.20** | 6.72 | 7.39 |
| `ink.tertiary` | `#5F5F68` | **5.30** | 5.75 | 6.32 |
| `state.ok` | `#12662F` | **5.94** | 6.43 | 7.08 |
| `state.warn` | `#7A4A00` | **6.28** | 6.80 | 7.48 |
| `state.fail` | `#A81E14` | **6.16** | 6.67 | 7.34 |
| `accent` | `#0A4FA0` | **6.68** | 7.24 | 7.96 |

**Worst cell across the whole system: 5.14:1** (`ink.tertiary`, dark, over a
white backdrop). That clears AA for normal text (4.5) with margin, and clears
AAA (7.0) for everything except tertiary and dark `state.fail` — acceptable,
since tertiary carries chrome and never a reading.

These numbers are why the scrim alphas are 0.94/0.92 and not something more
glassy. At dark α 0.86 the white-backdrop base rises to `#3C3C3E` and
`ink.tertiary` drops to **3.22** — a fail. At light α 0.80 the black-backdrop
base falls to `#CCCCCC` and `state.ok` drops to **3.15** — a fail. The
translucency budget is set by the contrast requirement, not by taste.

**Non-text minimum.** Data strokes, ring arcs and axis lines must clear **3:1**
against `surface.sunken`. All `state.*` and `accent` tokens do, by inspection of
the tables above (their worst is 5.14 against a *lighter* base than the sunken
well). `line.hair` at α.10 does not, and must not be used to carry data.

## 10. The plot primitive: one contract for every graphic

Today the sparkline, the air axis, the signal rings and the map star are four
independent drawing routines. They will diverge again unless there is one
primitive underneath. There is one, and it is deliberately small.

### 10.1 The contract

```
Plot {
    frame:   Bounds,          // the well; caller owns the size
    domain:  Scale,           // value -> unit interval, incl. its own clamping
    series:  &[Series],
    marks:   MarkSet,
    ticks:   &[Tick],
    labels:  LabelPolicy,
    epistemic: Epistemics,    // §11 — the primitive draws this, callers do not
}
```

`Plot` draws, and only draws:

1. **The well.** `surface.sunken`, `radius.none`, inset from the card by
   `space.tight`. Every plot in the app has a well; that is what makes them
   read as siblings.
2. **The baseline.** One `stroke.data` line in `ink.tertiary` at the domain's
   zero (or at the ring's start angle, or at the star's centre). Always drawn,
   even with no data — this is what makes the empty state look designed (§12).
3. **Ticks.** Exactly the ticks given. The primitive never invents ticks.
4. **Series.** Line, band, bar or arc — see §10.3.
5. **Labels.** Per `LabelPolicy`, never overlapping (see §10.4).
6. **The epistemic overlay.** Per §11 — hatch, fade, dash, gap.

`Plot` does not: choose colours (the caller passes tokens), decide units, format
numbers, or handle interaction. Those are the caller's, so that the primitive
stays the same across four windows.

### 10.2 Ticks — one appearance everywhere

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Length | 4px (minor), 7px (major) |
| Stroke | `stroke.hair`, `ink.tertiary` |
| Direction | Outward from the baseline, always |
| Major cadence | Every 4th minor, or every labelled tick, whichever is sparser |
| Label offset | `space.tight` (4) from the tick's far end |
| Label style | `type.micro`, `ink.tertiary`, tabular |
| Max labels | `floor(axis_length / (label_width + 16))` — drop by priority, never by truncation |

The rings and the star use the same tick vocabulary rendered radially: a ring's
"ticks" are radial stubs of the same 4/7px length at the same weight; the star's
are the spoke stubs at each node's angle. Same length, same weight, same colour
— that alone will make a ring and a sparkline read as one family.

### 10.3 Mark types, and which surface uses which

| Mark | Geometry | Fill | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Line` | Polyline, `stroke.data`, round joins | none | Panel sparkline |
| `Band` | Area between two series | series colour at **α 0.18** | Sparkline min/max envelope; hypothesis range (§11.2) |
| `Bar` | Rect, min width 3px, gap 1px | series colour at α 0.85 | Air channel occupancy |
| `Arc` | Ring segment, `stroke.data` × 3 = 4.5px track | track `surface.sunken`, value series colour | Signal rings |
| `Node` | Circle r=`glyph_box/2`, `stroke.data` outline | `surface.raised` | Map star nodes |
| `Edge` | Line, `stroke.data` | none | Map star spokes |
| `Point` | Circle r=2.5 | series colour | Last sample of a line; a single-sample series |

Fixed constants, everywhere: fill alpha **0.18** for a band; **0.85** for a bar;
arc track thickness **4.5px**; point radius **2.5px**. A caller may not vary
these. A single-sample series draws as a `Point`, never as a 1px line — a line
of one sample is a claim about a trend we do not have.

### 10.4 Label placement, one policy

`LabelPolicy` has three values and no others:

- `Anchored` — labels tied to ticks, placed outward, dropped by priority when
  they would collide. Axis labels.
- `Terminal` — one label at the series' last point, placed to the right, or
  above if within 12px of the frame edge. Sparkline current value.
- `None` — the value is read from a companion figure elsewhere in the card.

Collision resolution is always "drop the lower-priority label", never "shrink the
font" and never "rotate". Rotated axis labels in a 264px well are unreadable and
they are the reason the air window currently looks unlike the others.

### 10.5 What each surface becomes under the contract

| Surface | Frame | Domain | Marks | Ticks | Labels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Panel sparkline | 264×36 | time × value, clamped to p95 | `Line` + `Band` | 4 minor on time, 1 major at "now" | `Terminal` |
| Air channel axis | window × 120 | channel number × occupancy | `Bar` | major at 1/6/11/36/44/149 | `Anchored` |
| Signal ring | 44×44 | −90…−30 dBm | `Arc` | 3 radial stubs at −80/−65/−50 | `None` |
| Map star | window | polar layout | `Node` + `Edge` | spoke stub per node | `Anchored`, priority = hop distance |

Today these are three unrelated implementations: the sparkline is flexbox bars
with its own colour ramp and no ticks at all (`ui.rs:1205`); the air axis is
absolutely-positioned divs with a real coordinate mapping and its own tick and
label-thinning machinery (`air.rs:1231`, `axis_ticks` at `air.rs:526`); the map
star is a `gpui::canvas` path stroke plus absolutely-positioned chips with no
axis whatsoever (`map.rs:768`). The contract's job is to make the *third* of
these impossible to write again.

### 10.6 Signal strength: stop encoding it as opacity

`weight()` (`air.rs:615`) maps −90…−35 dBm onto alpha 0.25…0.85 on
`theme.fg`, and uses 0.20 when RSSI was not reported. Three objections, and the
first is disqualifying:

1. **It fails contrast, by computation.** `ink.primary` at α 0.25 over the
   dark worst-case base `#2A2A2C` yields roughly **1.9:1** — below even the 3:1
   non-text floor of §9. A weak signal is currently rendered as a bar that is
   nearly invisible, which reads as *absent* rather than *weak*.
2. **It collides with the staleness channel.** §11.5 spends alpha on age.
   Alpha cannot carry both age and signal strength; if it carries signal, then
   a stale strong AP and a fresh weak one are the same pixel.
3. **α 0.20 for "not reported" is exactly the conflation §11.4 forbids** — an
   unreported RSSI is rendered as a very weak one.

Replacement: **signal strength is length or arc extent, never alpha.** In the
air axis the bar's *height* stays fixed and its opacity becomes constant
(`mark.BAR_ALPHA` 0.85); strength is shown by a 44×44 `Arc` ring in the row's
leading glyph box, and by the numeral in the row. An unreported RSSI draws the
`Arc`'s track only, with no value sweep, plus the ○ marker — visibly "no
measurement", not "weak".

## 11. Epistemics as form

This is the core. The project's rule is *silent wrong data is worse than no
data*. Today that rule is expressed as orange prose, which produces false
alarm and gets skimmed. The replacement: **hue says what was measured; form
says how well we know it.** The two axes are independent and both are always
present.

### 11.1 The four epistemic states

| State | Meaning | Carrier |
|---|---|---|
| `Fact` | Directly observed this sample | Solid stroke, full-alpha ink, no marker |
| `Inferred` | Derived from observations by a stated rule | Dashed stroke, ink at α 1.0, `~` marker |
| `Hypothesis` | One reading among several the data admits | Hatched fill, ink at α 1.0, `?` marker |
| `Absent` | Not measured | No stroke at all — the gap form of §11.4 |

The marker is a **glyph in a 16px box** (per §3.4) placed immediately left of
the value, in `ink.tertiary`, `type.micro`. It is 11px of chrome replacing what
is presently a paragraph.

### 11.2 Confidence, shown as width not words

A hypothesis is not a scalar; it is a range. Render it as one:

- On a plot: the `Band` mark at α 0.18 spanning the admissible range, with the
  point estimate as a **dashed** `Line` through it. Wide band = weak claim, and
  that is legible in a quarter-second without reading anything.
- On a value row: the point estimate in `ink.primary` followed by the range in
  `ink.tertiary` `type.micro`, e.g. `ch 6 ⟨1–11⟩`. Not "probably channel 6,
  though 1 and 11 are also consistent with the observed…".
- Where there is no numeric range — a categorical hypothesis such as an uplink
  identity — render **all candidates**, the leading one in `ink.primary` and the
  rest in `ink.tertiary`, in one line, ranked. Three candidates at most; a
  fourth becomes `+2 more`.

**Confidence is never a percentage** unless the daemon computes a real one.
A fabricated "87% confident" is exactly the quiet lie.

### 11.2b The existing prose, converted

Every warn-coloured caveat in the app today, and what replaces it. The register
is currently inconsistent in a way that is itself a bug: `air.rs` paints its
epistemic caveats `theme.warn`, while `map.rs` paints the *same* register
`theme.muted` (`map.rs:569`, `map.rs:906`). Under §8.2 the map is right and the
air window is wrong — a hypothesis is not a warning.

| Today | Becomes |
|---|---|
| `air.rs:1205` — the ~70-word warn paragraph beginning "Overlap is a HYPOTHESIS about where the bands sit, not measured interference…" | The `?` marker in the band section header, `ink.tertiary`; hatched fill on every overlap cell; the full text moves to the header's hover tooltip. Zero warn ink |
| `air.rs:752` — `"hypothesis: covers {} of our channel · {}"` in a cell | Hatched fill + `?` marker + the coverage figure. The word "hypothesis" is carried by the hatch |
| `air.rs:1200` — the `pairing_line` in warn | `~` marker (inferred) + the rule, in `ink.tertiary` `type.micro` |

| `air.rs:924` / `933` — "This daemon cannot collect the air." + two-sentence explanation | The **Unsupported** form: ⊘, rows at `ink.tertiary` α 0.70, refusal card of §12.2 carrying the daemon's own sentence verbatim |

### 11.3 The shape ladder (also the colourblind fallback)

Each state carries a shape, drawn in the 16px marker box:

| State | Shape | Rendered as |
|---|---|---|
| ok | Filled disc | ● |
| warn | Filled triangle | ▲ |
| fail | Filled square with a notch | ◼ |
| skip | Hollow ring | ○ |
| unsupported | Hollow ring with a slash | ⊘ |

Shape and hue are redundant by design. A greyscale screenshot of the panel is
still fully readable — which matters, because a screenshot of this panel is the
artefact that ends up in the argument.

### 11.4 Absence: four different nothings

The single most important distinction in this app, and today all four look
alike. Each gets a distinct form, none of which uses `state.warn`:

| Kind | What it means | Form | Text |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SKIP** | The probe ran and could not measure | Hollow ring ○, value slot shows `—` (em dash) at `ink.tertiary`, slot width preserved | `not measured` on hover only |
| **Quiet** | Deliberately withheld on the wire | Hollow ring ○ + a 1px `line.edge` strike-through of the value slot | `withheld (quiet)` |
| **Paused** | Operator bracketed the silence | The plot's well is filled with a 45° hatch at `line.hair`, and the paused interval is **bounded by two vertical `line.edge` rules** — the bracket is literal | `paused HH:MM→HH:MM` in `type.micro` centred in the hatch |
| **Unsupported** | This daemon cannot produce this kind | ⊘, the whole row at `ink.tertiary` α 0.70, no value slot at all | `daemon cannot report this` |

The last one is the `subscribe_or_widen` case from `CLAUDE.md`. It must never
look like SKIP, because "this daemon cannot produce that kind" and "that kind
has produced nothing yet" are different facts and the window must not conflate
them. Here they differ in shape (⊘ vs ○), in whether a value slot exists at all,
and in row alpha.

### 11.5 Staleness: readings fade, alarms do not

A reading has an age. Past the collector's tick interval it is no longer a claim
about *now*.

| Age (× tick interval) | Ink alpha | Extra |
|---|---|---|
| < 1.5× | 1.00 | — |
| 1.5–3× | 0.85 | — |
| 3–6× | 0.70 | Age chip appears: `12s` in `type.micro` |
| > 6× | — | The value is **replaced by the SKIP form** (§11.4), and the last value moves into the hover tooltip |

Two hard rules, both derived by computation not taste:

- **The alpha floor is 0.70.** Computed against the worst-case bases: dark
  `ink.primary` at α 0.70 gives **7.09:1**, light gives **5.77:1** — both clear
  AA. At α 0.55 light drops to **3.65:1** and fails. So 0.70 is the floor, and
  below it the value must be withdrawn rather than dimmed further.
- **`state.*` colours never fade.** Computed: dark `state.fail` at α 0.70 is
  **3.52:1** and light is **3.67:1** — both fail AA. More importantly, a faded
  red is a half-hearted alarm, which is meaningless. Instead, **a stale reading
  loses its state colour entirely** and reverts to `ink.primary` at the age
  alpha. The state colour *is* the assertion "we measured this, now"; if that is
  no longer true the colour must go. This is the single rule that most directly
  implements "silent wrong data is worse than no data": stale amber stops
  shouting and starts saying "this is old".

Ages are computed from the sample's `ts_us`, never from the render clock's own
notion of freshness.

## 12. Empty, refused, and working

Three distinct views. None of them is a spinner over a blank window.

### 12.1 Empty — the instrument is fine, there is nothing to show

The layout **stays**. Axes, wells, ticks, row labels, column headers all render
exactly as they would with data; only values are the SKIP form (`—`). A single
`type.body` `ink.secondary` line sits centred in the largest well:

> `no neighbours seen yet`

Never centred in the *window* — centred in the well, so the surrounding
structure remains visible and the user can see *what would appear where*.

Rationale: an empty state that removes the layout is indistinguishable from a
crash, which is precisely the reported complaint. Keeping the skeleton is also
honest: it shows the instrument's shape and where the hole is.

Today `empty_state()` (`map.rs:1105`) draws a single `py_1` muted line and
nothing else; the sparkline's empty case (`ui.rs:1219`) replaces the whole plot
with the words `no data`; and the events window has **no empty state at all** —
an empty `uniform_list` renders as a blank rectangle (`events.rs`). All three
become the skeleton form. In particular the sparkline keeps its well, its
baseline and its threshold rule and shows 20 `—` columns; a plot that vanishes
when it has nothing to say is the exact shape of the complaint.

The existing strings are good and should survive: `no neighbour reading yet`,
`no neighbours seen · press Scan to look`, `no recent incidents`. They are
precise about *which* nothing this is, which is the §11.4 distinction expressed
in words already — the work is to give them a form.

### 12.2 Refused — the daemon or the OS said no

Three parts, in order, in a card at `surface.raised`:

1. `⊘` marker + `type.strong` one-line statement of what could not be done:
   `cannot read neighbour table`
2. `type.body` `ink.secondary`: **the daemon's own message, verbatim**, never a
   deserializer's complaint (this is what `decode_handshake` exists for).
3. `type.micro` `ink.tertiary`: what the operator can do, one line, or nothing.
   Never speculation about causes.

Colour: `ink` and `line.edge` only. A refusal is **not** `state.fail` — the
network may be perfectly healthy; it is our instrument that is blind. Painting a
refusal red is the same category error as painting a hypothesis orange.

### 12.3 Working — something is in flight

- **Under 400 ms: draw nothing.** Most reads finish inside this and a flash of
  spinner is worse than a still frame.
- **400 ms – 3 s:** the affected values become the SKIP form and a 2px
  `accent` progress hairline runs along the **bottom edge of the affected card
  only** — never a modal, never a whole-window overlay. Indeterminate: a 25%-wide
  segment traversing the width in 1200 ms, linear.
- **Beyond 3 s:** the hairline stays and a `type.micro` `ink.tertiary` line
  appears: `waiting on daemon · 4s`. The counter is tabular (§4) so it does not
  shake.
- **First launch with no connection yet:** this is not "working", it is §12.2
  refused, with the socket path named.

The scan button's existing label ladder — `Scan now` → `Asking…` →
`Scanning… (a few seconds)` → `Scan unavailable` (`air.rs:1104`) — is kept as
is. It is already the honest form: it distinguishes "the daemon has not answered
yet" from "the daemon accepted and the radio is being read", which is the same
distinction §11.4 draws for absence. The progress hairline attaches to the card
under it; the labels do not change.

Stale data is **never** blanked while a refresh is in flight. It fades per
§11.5 and keeps its age chip. A window that goes blank to reload has destroyed
evidence.

## 13. Motion

The instrument's job is to let a number be trusted. Motion that makes a value
harder to read exactly at the moment it changes is anti-functional.

### 13.1 Permitted

| Motion | Duration | Curve | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Panel appear/dismiss | 140 ms | ease-out / ease-in | Opacity + 6px Y offset |
| Flyout menu slide | 160 ms | ease-out | X offset only |
| Hover/press surface tint | 90 ms | linear | `surface.hover`/`active` |
| Focus ring | 0 ms | — | Instant. A focus ring that animates is a focus ring you miss |
| Staleness fade | 600 ms | linear | The alpha steps of §11.5 only |
| Progress hairline | 1200 ms loop | linear | §12.3 |
| Plot data update | 250 ms | ease-out | **Geometry only** — see below |

### 13.2 Forbidden, and why

- **Animating a numeral.** No count-up, no odometer, no cross-fade of digits.
  A number in transition is a number that is briefly *wrong on screen*. It
  changes instantly, in place, in its fixed slot (§4).
- **Animating colour between `state.*` values.** A value mid-way between green
  and red asserts a state that was never measured. State colour switches on the
  same frame the value does.
- **Any looping, pulsing, or breathing indicator of health.** A pulsing green
  dot is an animation that carries no information and trains the eye to ignore
  the one region that should command it.
- **Spinners over content.** See §12.3.
- **Reflow animation.** When a list gains or loses a row, the rows do not slide.
  Layout is instant; only opacity of the new row animates (140 ms).
- **Anything over 300 ms** except the two explicitly listed loops. A 3-second
  refresh cycle cannot afford a 500 ms transition — the next update arrives
  before the eye has settled.
- **Parallax, scale-on-hover, shadow-lift.** No decorative depth.

### 13.3 The plot update rule

On new data, a plot animates its **geometry** (points move to new positions,
bars change height) over 250 ms ease-out, but **never** its colour, its label
text, or its epistemic overlay — those switch instantly. The reason: geometry
motion carries information (you can see the direction of change), while colour
and text motion carries only ambiguity.

`prefers-reduced-motion` (via `NSWorkspace.accessibilityDisplayShouldReduceMotion`):
all durations collapse to 0 except the progress hairline, which becomes a static
25% `accent` segment at the card's leading edge.

right-aligned numeral slot; `ms` in a fixed `type.micro` `ink.tertiary` slot.
Nothing moves. Value colour is `state.ok`. On the tick after the daemon goes
quiet the numeral becomes `—`, the shape marker becomes ○, and the row's colour
drops to `ink.tertiary` — instantly readable as "not measured", not as "zero".

**The air window's channel axis.**
*Was:* absolutely-positioned divs with their own tick machinery (`axis_ticks`,
`TICK_H 4`, `TICK_LABEL_HALF_W 11`, `MIN_TICK_LABEL_GAP 24`), bar height 9px,
and signal strength carried by fill alpha from 0.25 to 0.85 — the weak end
sitting at roughly 1.9:1 against its own background.
*Becomes:* the same well, baseline and tick vocabulary as the panel sparkline
(4/7px stubs, `ink.tertiary`, `type.micro` tabular labels); `Bar` marks at a
constant α 0.85; strength moved into a leading `Arc` ring and a tabular numeral.
A band the daemon could not measure the strength of draws the ring's track with
no sweep and a ○ — where today it draws a bar at α 0.20 that reads as "very
weak".

**The overlap caveat.**
*Was:* about seventy words of `theme.warn` beginning "Overlap is a HYPOTHESIS
about where the bands sit, not measured interference…", standing above the
bands, read once and skipped forever after.
*Becomes:* a `?` in a 16px box beside the section header; every overlap cell
filled with the hatch of §11.1; the paragraph, unchanged, in the header's
tooltip. The claim is now visible on each individual cell it applies to instead
of being asserted once and forgotten — which is strictly *more* honest, at about
2% of the ink.

**A stale RSSI reading.**
*Was:* keeps showing `−58 dBm` in green indefinitely; the operator cannot tell
whether it is live.
*Becomes:* at 1.5 ticks it drops to α 0.85 and loses the green (it is no longer
a claim about now); at 3 ticks an `18s` age chip appears; at 6 ticks the number
is replaced by `—` with a ○, and `−58 dBm @ 12:04:31` moves to the tooltip.

**An empty neighbour table.**
*Was:* a collapsed window with a stray border and no content — reads as a crash.
*Becomes:* the star's spokes, the ring ticks and the column headers all render;
values are `—`; one `ink.secondary` line in the well says `no neighbours seen
yet`. The user can see the instrument is fine and the network is quiet.

**A pre-air daemon refusing an `Air` subscription.**
*Was:* an orange paragraph, or a blank window.
*Becomes:* the air window renders its full skeleton; every row is `⊘` at
`ink.tertiary` α 0.70 with no value slot; a `surface.raised` card carries
`cannot report air` and, verbatim, the daemon's own message. Wi-Fi rows in the
panel are unaffected and stay live — the widened subscription's client-side
filtering is *visible* as those rows continuing to update.

## 16. Open questions for the owner

These are the calls I would most expect to be overruled on, listed so they can
be argued rather than discovered later.

1. **Stale readings lose their state colour** (§11.5). This means an alarm that
   goes stale stops being red. That is defensible ("red asserts a live
   measurement") and it is also the opposite of what most monitoring tools do —
   they keep the alarm hot until acknowledged. If the owner wants a failure to
   keep shouting after the daemon goes quiet, the whole staleness ladder changes.
2. **A refusal is not `state.fail`** (§12.2). Blindness of the instrument is
   coloured as ink, not as alarm. Arguable the other way: an observer that
   cannot observe is, for this project, an emergency.
3. **Scrim at α 0.94 / 0.92** (§8.1). This is a nearly-opaque panel. It buys
   guaranteed contrast over any wallpaper and it substantially reduces the glass
   look the owner may have wanted from `NSVisualEffectView`. The alternative is
   a lower alpha plus a per-token contrast fallback measured at runtime, which is
   more code and unverifiable on this machine. Note the current `surface` alpha
   is `0xf2` ≈ 0.95, bisected by hand against a native `NSMenu` (`ui.rs:138`) —
   so this proposal is *close to* what is already shipping, and the visible
   change would be the three ordinary windows losing their opaque `bg`.

Two further calls, slightly less contentious but worth naming:

4. **Signal strength stops being opacity** (§10.6). This changes the air
   window's whole look — the current alpha-graded bars are its most distinctive
   feature. The argument for the change is a computed contrast failure (≈1.9:1
   at the weak end), not taste, but the owner may prefer to keep the encoding
   and raise the alpha floor instead.
5. **`state.warn` survives in about four places** (§11.2b). Roughly sixteen of
   the twenty current warn-coloured sites lose their colour. If the owner reads
   any of those as genuine warnings rather than epistemic caveats, that site
   should be argued individually.

### Not verifiable here

- Every claim in this document about *what the operator sees* falls under the
  Ceiling in `CLAUDE.md`: the agent can compile the bar but cannot run it.
  Contrast ratios are computed and therefore checkable; perceived legibility of
  11px tabular figures on this machine's actual displays is not.
- Whether gpui's text shaping exposes `tnum` on the system face, or whether §4
  requires switching the numeric slots to SF Mono, was not verified against the
  gpui version pinned here. An implementer must check this first; it is the
  load-bearing assumption of §4.
- The 264px inner plot column assumes the panel's 16px gutters and 12px card
  padding survive implementation. If the panel width or gutters change, every
